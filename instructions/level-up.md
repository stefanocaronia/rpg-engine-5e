Each new level gives hit points, but a new character's first, which takes the largest number of its class's hit die: for each, roll the hit die of the class that rose (the player for the player character, in choices with the dashboard, else with ask, even when they would leave it to you; you for a companion), or take its average (d6 4, d8 5, d10 6, d12 7), and add them to hp-base with write, without con-mod, which the system adds by itself. Then go through the new features and the columns that grew (show Page#Feature): the choices they ask, such as an Ability Score Improvement, a subclass or the spells a wizard adds to the spellbook, are the player's for the player character, yours for a companion unless the player made it, written in their lists. A first level in another class follows rules "Multiclassing", with its prerequisites and the few proficiencies it gives.
With the dashboard, prepare one level at a time with choices, from the row of the class that rises at its own level, never the character's: the hit die, as an option that rolls it and one of its average; only what that row names, a subclass only where the class first names it; spells only of the levels that class has slots for (a warlock's Slot Level), as many as its columns grew (a wizard, two for the spellbook), the whole list of its class's spells as links (show Wizard Spells for a wizard) but those the character has, never your selection; for a class that knows spells, an item with pick 0-1 to swap one; an Ability Score Improvement as an item of the abilities below 20 with pick 1-2, or 0-2 when the table plays feats: one chosen takes +2, two take +1 each; with feats, before it an item with the improvement and the feats as pages, and none chosen with a feat. Another class only when the player asks: rules "Multiclassing", then choices again.
